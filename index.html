Smart Tourism

Integrating Environmental, Behavioral, and Digital Data for Adaptive Forecasting.

Explore the Research View Resources

Research Domain

Understanding the context, identifying the gaps, and formulating a robust methodology to tackle the challenges of modern system restoration.

Literature Survey

Tourism systems are becoming increasingly complex and volatile, influenced heavily by a combination of environmental conditions, visitor behavior, digital sentiment, and socio-political events. Current tourism management and planning tools largely rely on static, historical data and lack the responsiveness needed to operate effectively in real time. While sentiment analysis utilizing social media and online reviews has emerged as a powerful tool for predicting tourist interest, these inputs are often used in isolation rather than being cross-referenced with real-world crowd data or contextual environmental changes. Furthermore, most machine learning models used in this domain operate as black boxes, lacking the feedback loops required to correct or improve predictions after they are made.

Research Gap

Current forecasting tools suffer from a limited integration of diverse, real-time data streams—such as live visitor footfall, dynamic weather changes, and live public sentiment. A major limitation in existing models is the lack of visibility into the difference between forecasted expectations and actual, on-the-ground conditions. There is a distinct absence of a live, corrective feedback mechanism capable of continuously refining and retraining AI models based on actual observations, which makes current systems brittle and unable to adapt to sudden disruptions.

Research Problem

Tourism authorities, destination managers, and travelers struggle to accurately anticipate overcrowding, understand shifting public perception, and adapt to real-time weather or event disruptions. Without a dynamic system that integrates real-time conditions and self-corrects its predictions based on live feedback, planning remains inaccurate, particularly during peak seasons or unexpected crises.

Research Objectives

→ Main Objective: Develop an AI-driven, feedback-corrected tourism flow prediction framework by integrating sentiment analysis, environmental forecasts, and historical data with real-time observations from a Digital Twin platform to enable continuous model fine-tuning.
→ Develop a Core AI Prediction Model utilizing time-series and regression-based machine learning to forecast future tourist flows.
→ Develop a Digital Twin Platform for real-time monitoring of crowd density, weather, and location-based sentiment, providing "actual" data for system feedback.
→ Implement a Sentiment Analysis Engine leveraging NLP (e.g., transformer architectures) to extract public perception signals and emotional tones from user-generated content.
→ Create a Context-Aware Personalized Tour Planning system that uses AI scoring and greedy scheduling algorithms to recommend and dynamically adapt optimal routes.

Methodology

We propose a hybrid AI-driven, feedback-corrected framework. The solution begins with a Core AI Prediction Model that estimates expected tourist flows using historical data, weather forecasts, and real-time outputs from a Sentiment Analysis Engine (which uses NLP models like BERT to process social media and review data).
Simultaneously, a Digital Twin Platform acts as a live virtual replica of the tourism environment, mapping actual crowd footfall, live weather, and current sentiment. A critical Feedback Correction Module continuously compares the AI's predicted values against the Digital Twin's actual observed values. Any discrepancies are used to fine-tune and retrain the core model via incremental learning, allowing the system to self-correct. Finally, these dynamic insights power a Personalized Tour Planning System that adjusts itineraries in real time based on these contextual inputs
